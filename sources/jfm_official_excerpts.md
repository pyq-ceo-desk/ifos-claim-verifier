# Official JFM excerpts (MoEF circular 1 June 1990 / strengthening guidelines — demo corpus)

## MoEF circular, 1 June 1990 (Joint Forest Management)
- The Government of India encouraged State Governments to involve village communities in the protection and development of degraded forest lands.
- Village institutions (often called Forest Protection Committees / Village Forest Committees) may be constituted for this purpose.
- Beneficiaries are entitled to usufructs such as grasses, lops and tops of branches, and non-timber forest produce, and a share in the proceeds from final harvest as per State resolutions.
- The Forest Department retains ownership of the land; communities share management responsibility for regeneration and protection.

## Strengthening JFM guidelines (2000/2002 public framing)
- Emphasis on microplans prepared with community participation.
- Greater attention to women's representation and disadvantaged groups in management committees.
- Convergence with other rural development and watershed programmes is encouraged.
- Benefit-sharing arrangements should be clear, transparent and time-bound in State JFM resolutions.

## Limits (what JFM is not)
- JFM does not by itself transfer legal title of reserved forest land to the village committee.
- Commercial clear-felling of natural forests is outside the protective/regenerative purpose of classical JFM on degraded lands.
